# AI Landscape Newsletter - Edition #4
## Week of December 30, 2025

---

**My Take**

As we close 2025, one pattern stands out: the technology arrived faster than our readiness to use it well. McKinsey's new research confirms that design thinking, mentorship, and ethical judgment remain "uniquely human"—but that's only valuable if we practice those skills ourselves first. Before we design AI-powered learning for others, we need to experiment with our own workflows. Before we teach learners to question AI outputs, we need to build that muscle in our teams. 2026 calls for intentional spaces—for reflection, for cross-functional pollination, for checking our own design biases. The organizations that thrive won't just adopt AI; they'll grow alongside it with humility and intention. That's the work ahead.

**Workforce & Skills**

**Agents, Robots, and Us: Skill Partnerships in the Age of AI** | [Read](https://www.mckinsey.com/mgi/our-research/agents-robots-and-us-skill-partnerships-in-the-age-of-ai)
McKinsey's latest research identifies which skills AI will augment vs. replace—design thinking, mentorship, and ethical judgment remain uniquely human. For EdTech teams, this frames exactly what our learning programs should prioritize as AI handles routine tasks.

**2026 L&D Report: AI Adoption Outpacing Workforce Readiness** | [Read](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/12/04/3199968/0/en/New-2026-L-D-Report-Shows-AI-Adoption-Outpacing-Workforce-Readiness-Critical-Thinking-Leadership-and-Mentorship-Needs.html)
New data confirms the readiness gap: organizations are deploying AI faster than employees can adapt, with critical thinking and leadership skills lagging most. This validates the urgency of intentional upskilling programs over passive AI exposure.

**AI Research & Models**

**Stanford AI Experts Predict What Will Happen in 2026** | [Read](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/stanford-ai-experts-predict-what-will-happen-in-2026)
Stanford HAI researchers forecast 2026 will bring more capable AI agents, continued regulatory uncertainty, and widening gaps between AI-ready and AI-lagging organizations. Worth reading for strategic planning and anticipating where learner needs will shift.

**What to Expect from AI in 2026?** | [Read](https://www.humanintheloop.online/p/28-edition-what-to-expect-from-ai-in-2026)
Human in the Loop's year-end analysis highlights that 2026 will test whether organizations can translate AI capability into actual productivity—the "readiness gap" theme. Reinforces that skills development, not just tool access, determines outcomes.

**EdTech Spotlight**

**AI Tutoring Outperforms Active Learning (Harvard RCT)** | [Read](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6)
A Harvard randomized controlled trial found AI tutoring produced learning gains exceeding traditional active learning methods. This is significant evidence for AI-assisted instruction—but raises questions about what "active learning" means when AI does the adapting.

**AI and the Next Digital Divide in Education** | [Read](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/ai-and-the-next-digital-divide-in-education/)
Brookings warns that AI in education risks creating new inequities: students with AI access and literacy will surge ahead while others fall further behind. Essential framing for any EdTech equity strategy in 2026.
