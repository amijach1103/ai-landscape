AI LANDSCAPE | Week of December 10

My Take

The OECD report confirms what we see daily: AI training overwhelmingly targets people who already have technical skills, leaving our learners (those furthest from opportunity) behind. But here's what's interesting: new research from Perplexity and Harvard shows that people who use AI agents for learning and research become the most engaged long-term users. Our advantage as learning designers and researchers? We can move faster than big players by staying close to learners. Design thinking (empathy first, rapid iteration, bias toward testing) lets us validate what works for our populations. The Danish Futures Lab model is worth watching: students solving real company challenges while building foresight skills. That's the kind of integration we should be prototyping.

Workforce & Skills

MIT: AI Can Replace 11.7% of U.S. Workforce
The "Iceberg Index" simulation finds 17.7 million jobs ($1.2T in wages) are technically and economically replaceable by AI today, concentrated in administrative, financial, and HR roles. This directly informs which skills we should be building into our programs, and which we shouldn't.

OECD: Bridging the AI Skills Gap
Only 0.3-5.5% of training courses deliver AI content despite 1-in-3 jobs having high AI exposure; most training targets already-skilled professionals. This is a direct market signal for accessible, entry-level AI literacy programs.

Tools & Applications

How People Actually Use AI Agents (Perplexity + Harvard Study)
First large-scale study of real-world agent usage: 57% of activity is cognitive work (productivity, learning, research), not just booking hotels. Users who start with learning/research tasks become long-term users. This is empirical proof we're entering a "hybrid intelligence economy."

OpenAI Launches First Certification Courses
Two new courses: "AI Foundations" for workers (piloting with Walmart, Deere, Lowe's) and "ChatGPT Foundations for Teachers" (on Coursera). The goal is to certify 10 million Americans by 2030. This is direct competition in our space: OpenAI is linking learning to certification to jobs platform, with ChatGPT as tutor, practice space, and feedback loop.

Futures Lab: Teaching Students to Shape the Future
Danish program partners students with companies like LEGO and IKEA to develop "futures literacy" through scenario analysis, trend mapping, and systems thinking. Students work on real challenges and present solutions to partner organizations. A model for how we might integrate foresight skills into workforce development.
